# L23: bot-exit detection on held positions (SHADOW first)

BASE: `origin/integration/r1` (live in paper). Branch `cloud/L23`. Paper only; `desk/` untouched; nothing signs or sends.
All logic lives in `lean/bot_exit.py`. The shared files get small, delimited `L23` hooks:

- `runner.py`:
  - `self.bot_exit = None` in `__init__`;
  - one `trigger()` lookup at the top of `_manage`, plus `bot_exit=bool(bot)` on its two `exit_decision` calls;
  - `health['bot_exit']` (through `_aux_health`, so `alive` is included);
  - the daemon thread in `run()`, joined within the shared LINT2b aux deadline.
- `strategy.py`: `exit_decision(..., bot_exit=False)`. This is a forced full exit with reason `BOT_EXIT`, ranked after LIQUIDATE and DANGER and before STOP. False changes nothing.
- `__main__.py`: the `bot_exit` config key (strict load) and `build_runner` wiring.
- `report.py`: a `bot_exit` section and its HTML.
- `config/lean/lean.example.json`: a `bot_exit` block in **shadow** mode.

## Why

Live data shows two patterns:
- Most realized losses are gap dumps. The price falls from about entry to −80 % between two 10 s marks, and the stop cannot fill.
- Most winners ride bot-pumped staircases.

The hypothesis is that the dump is preceded by signals we can observe. This module watches every held pool for those signals. In shadow mode it only records what it would have done.

## Design

**Thread and lanes.**
- The monitor runs in its own daemon thread (`run_loop`, every `poll_s`) and never on the exit thread.
- It uses only the shared non-blocking LOW lane (`providers.low`).
- The runner's side is `trigger(mint, open_fill_id)`. It is a dict read under a tiny lock that is never held across I/O, and it returns something only in live mode.

**One pass.** It reads `store.positions()` and `position_states()`, keeps one `Watch` per lifecycle `(mint, open_fill_id)`, and forgets closed lifecycles. It then makes these calls, all within `max_calls_per_pass`:

1. **Signatures.** `getSignaturesForAddress(pool, {limit: sig_limit, until: last seen})` per position, in round-robin order. This gives an exact count of pool transactions with their block times.
   - A full page leaves a gap.
   - Any window that overlaps a gap, or starts before the first page, gets **no rate metric**. It is never read as "the bot stopped".
2. **Sampled swaps.** `getTransaction` (jsonParsed) for `sample_txs` NEW signatures per position, spread over the page with the newest included.
   - Each sample is parsed from the pool's own token balances: side, SOL in or out of its wSOL vault, the trader, and the quote reserve before and after.
   - When the budget is short, the positions whose tx-rate ratio is falling are sampled first.
3. **Holders.** Every `holders_every` passes, `getTokenLargestAccounts(mint)` (pool vault excluded) gives the outflow of the top holders since the last check.

**Pacing and sheds.**
- Calls are paced `pace_s` apart on the monitor's own thread.
- A call is skipped when the low lane would keep fewer than `reserve_tokens` tokens for the path recorder, L11, L12F and L13 (this is a peek; nothing is taken).
- `LANE_SHED`, a yield, or a spent budget ends the pass immediately. Nothing waits.
- Any other provider error or malformed body is isolated to that position: an `errors` row with scope `bot_exit`.

**Metrics.** `window_s` is the "30 s" window. `baseline_s` is the window *before* it.

| metric | source |
|---|---|
| `tx_rate_30s`, `tx_rate_baseline`, `tx_rate_ratio`, `failed_share_30s` | signatures. Exact and gap-aware. Failed txs are not counted as activity. The baseline needs `min_baseline_tx` txs, so a quiet pool has no "bot rate" to lose. |
| `sell_share_30s` (needs `min_sample` samples), `net_sol_flow_30s`, `max_sell_reserve_frac`, `buy_size_ratio`, `pumper_net_sol_30s`, `pumper_sellers_30s`, `creator_sold`, `swaps_sampled_30s` | sampled swaps |
| `top_holders_sold_frac` | holder checks |

- `net_sol_flow_30s` is the change in the reserve itself, from the oldest to the newest sample in the window.
- **pumpers** are wallets with a sampled BUY in the first `pumper_window_s` (120 s) after entry.
- **creator** is the PumpSwap pool's `coin_creator`, read from the pool account the screen retained (no request).
- A metric without enough evidence is `None`, and a condition on `None` never holds.

**Rules.** The rules are data-driven: `rules: [{name, all: [{metric, op (< <= > >=), value (decimal string)}]}]`. A signal fires when every condition of any rule holds. There is one signal per lifecycle, and it is sticky; it is replayed from the store on restart. The defaults are:

| rule | conditions |
|---|---|
| `BOT_STOPPED` | `tx_rate_ratio < 0.3` AND `sell_share_30s > 0.6` |
| `WHALE_SELL` | `max_sell_reserve_frac > 0.15` |
| `CREATOR_SELL` | `creator_sold >= 1` |
| `PUMPERS_OUT` | `pumper_net_sol_30s < 0` AND `pumper_sellers_30s >= 2` AND `tx_rate_ratio < 0.6` |

`PUMPERS_OUT` is anchored on the exact rate. A flow estimate from about 6 samples alone turned negative in the bot's ordinary churn (70 % buys): about 7 % of evaluations, so it fired within minutes in the e2e. The task's literal rule is still expressible in config.

**SHADOW (default).**
- It writes `decisions(kind='shadow_exit', action='SIGNAL', reasons=[rules])` with these features: `mode`, `open_fill_id`, `signal_at`, all metrics, `qty_raw`, `value_lamports`, `value_source`, `pumpers`, `creator_known`, `held_s`.
- The hypothetical value is a paper sell of the whole quantity, with the same slippage haircut and fixed fee as `lean.paper`. Sources in order:
  1. a D1 quote mark younger than `quote_ttl_s`;
  2. else ONE Jupiter sell quote on the low lane;
  3. else a fresh vault mark;
  4. else unvalued.
- Trading is NOT changed. The e2e shows the fills are byte-identical to a run without the key.

**LIVE (`mode: "live"`, implemented, OFF).**
- It writes `decisions(kind='bot_exit')`, and the trigger is armed.
- `_manage` then wants the position out with reason `BOT_EXIT` through the ordinary exit path: with L10 on, the two-phase q0 → delay → q1. It uses the same quote, fee, slippage, D2 failure clock and write-off.
- A live signal survives a restart.
- An older *shadow* signal never becomes an exit after switching to live.

**Report** (`summary['bot_exit']`, HTML "Bot-exit signals"). For each signal of a closed lifecycle it compares:
- the signal value; and
- the economic proceeds of every sell of that lifecycle *after* the signal (sol − fee, refundable ATA rent removed).

It reports:
- `saved_count` / `cost_count`, the sums, `saved_sum_sol`;
- `false_positive_rate`: the share of scored signals where holding fetched more;
- open and unvalued counts, and counts by rule and by mode;
- closed trades without a signal, and big losses (≤ −50 %) and how many of them were signalled.

| key | default | meaning |

| `mode` | `"shadow"` | or `"live"` |
| `poll_s` | 10 | seconds between passes |
| `window_s` | 30 | the recent window |
| `baseline_s` | 180 | the window before it |
| `min_baseline_tx` | 30 | |
| `min_sample` | 4 | |
| `pumper_window_s` | 120 | |
| `sig_limit` | 300 | |
| `sample_txs` | 2 | per position per pass |
| `holders_every` | 3 | 0 = off |
| `max_calls_per_pass` | 6 | |
| `reserve_tokens` | 1 | |

| `quote_ttl_s` | 10 | |

Loading is strict: unknown keys, unknown metrics or ops, float or NaN values, duplicate rule names and out-of-range integers are all refused at `load_config`.

## Call budget

Helius calls, all on the LOW lane, hard-capped at `max_calls_per_pass` per `poll_s`:

| holdings | calls | Helius rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 position | 1 sig + 2 tx + ⅓ holders ≈ **3.3 calls / 10 s** | ≈ 0.33 req/s, ≈ 28.8 k calls/day while held |
| 2 or more positions | the cap: **6 calls / 10 s** | ≈ 0.6 req/s, ≤ 51.8 k calls/day while held |

- With nothing held, nothing is sent.
- The low lane is about 2 req/s (20 % of 10/s). Pacing 0.5 s plus the 1-token reserve keeps bursts off the other users.
- The L15 credit tracker counts these calls at the transport (1 credit each by the shipped `credit_costs`). Archival methods (`getTransaction`, `getSignaturesForAddress`) may be priced higher on the real plan: **calibrate against the Helius dashboard.**
- When the tracker sheds the low lane over budget, this monitor simply skips.

Jupiter: one sell quote per shadow signal (at most one per lifecycle), on the low lane, and none in live mode.

At `max_positions` 4, the budget is 4 signature calls + 2 samples per pass. Only the falling-rate pools get samples, which the prioritization test proves. Raise `max_calls_per_pass` for denser sampling.

## Tests / evidence

`tests/lean/test_bot_exit.py`: 34 tests, real lean modules, only HTTP faked, plus a synthetic pool tx stream `Flow` behind `getSignaturesForAddress` / `getTransaction`.

End to end:
- **Bot pump, then stop, then gap dump.**
  - The bot runs at 5 tx/s with 70 % buys on a staircase. At +900 s it stops (0.5 tx/s, 90 % sells); at +1000 s the price gaps to 0.2.
  - Shadow records `BOT_STOPPED` at +930 s, before the dump (rate ratio 0.10, sell share 0.83).
  - The fills are IDENTICAL to a run without the key: the same STOP after the gap.
  - The shadow value is exactly a paper sell of the held quantity at the low-lane quote.
  - The report shows saved 1, cost 0, FP rate 0.0000, and 1 of 1 big losses signalled.
- **Live, with L10 two-phase exits on.** The position exits as `BOT_EXIT` on the next position pass (q1 fill, q0 recorded), before the gap, for more than 3× the stop's proceeds.
- **Restarts.**
  - A live trigger survives a restart (the process dies right after the signal pass; the restarted runner exits `BOT_EXIT` before the gap and does not signal twice).
  - Switching shadow → live does not turn the old shadow signal into an exit.
- **Noise pool** (2 tx/s, 50/50, many wallets): no signal over 1300 s, with more than 50 polls and more than 50 swaps parsed.
- **Other rules and the report.**
  - In a quiet pool, a creator sell gives `CREATOR_SELL`. When holding later paid more, the report counts it as a false positive (FP rate 1.0000).
  - A 25 % sell gives `WHALE_SELL`, and the quiet pool has no rate metric.
  - A custom rule from config fires.

Budget, shed and isolation:
- Each pass stays within its budget, on the low lane.
- A shed lane makes no call and costs no time, both through the peek and through the `LANE_SHED` answer.
- A permanently shed lane gives fills byte-identical to today, in live mode too.
- The reserve is respected.
- A malformed provider body is isolated: an `errors` row, and the position is kept.
- Failed txs are excluded from the rate.
- The hook does no I/O.
- The thread runs and reports `alive`, `signals`, `calls`, `sheds`.
- With 2 positions and a budget of 4, the falling-rate pool gets the samples.

Config:
- Absent or disabled means off. Loading is strict.
- The example turns on shadow only.
- **The production r1 config (without `bot_exit`) loads unchanged with bot_exit OFF.** Adding exactly the example block turns on shadow.
- `python -m lean --once` with the example builds the monitor and never starts it (0 calls).

Pure tests cover the parsers, rule evaluation (None never holds; strict `<`), holder outflow, sampling spread, gap and coverage logic, and `min_sample`.

Mutants (one replacement each, all killed):
- shadow arming a trigger; the trigger ignoring the mode; the exit reason DANGER; the runner ignoring the trigger;
- a trigger outliving its position; a live signal lost on restart; a shadow signal becoming a live exit; a lifecycle signalling twice;
- unknown metric holds; gaps ignored; coverage ignored; a full page leaving no gap; failed txs counted; a quiet baseline having a rate; sell share on too few samples;
- whale fraction of the wrong reserve; anyone being the creator; anyone being a pumper; sampling dropping the newest;
- samples not prioritised, or prioritised backwards; no pass budget; reserve ignored; shed not counted;
- no hypothetical quote; value without slippage; report saved/cost swapped; false positives not counted.

## Limitations

- **Sampling.**
  - With a bot at 13 tx/s, 2 samples per 10 s parse about 1.5 % of swaps.
  - A single whale or creator sell during heavy bot traffic is usually missed. `WHALE_SELL` and `CREATOR_SELL` are reliable only once traffic thins.
  - The tx rate is exact. The sell share, sizes and pumper flow are estimates, so every default rule is anchored on the exact rate or on a single large sell.
- **Timing.**
  - A signal is evaluated at most every `poll_s`.
  - In live mode the exit happens on the next position pass: up to 10 s, plus the L10 delay of 3 s. A gap within about 13 s of the signal is not avoided.
  - `blockTime` has 1 s granularity, and confirmation lag biases the recent rate about 3 % low.
- **Valuation.**
  - The shadow value is a q0-like quote, without the L10 latency tax that a real exit would pay. "Saved" is optimistic by that tax.
  - Unvalued signals are those where the low lane was shed and there was no fresh mark. They are counted, not scored.
- **Holders.** The holder check sees the top-20 token accounts. Only the pool's base vault is excluded; burn or locker accounts are not identified.
- **Pumpers.** "Pumpers" are early sampled buyers, not a funding-cluster analysis (L13 owns clustering).
- **Restarts.** After a restart, a watch restarts cold. The first signature page (300) rebuilds about 60 s of history at 5 tx/s, so the rate metric is `None` until about 3.5 min of record exists. This is conservative.
- **Live-mode cooldown.** A `BOT_EXIT` close gets the normal `cooldown_seconds`, not the stop cooldown.
- **Untuned defaults.** The default thresholds are the task's examples and are untuned. Shadow data and the report section are how to tune them before anyone considers `mode: "live"`.
- **Unverified shapes.** The response shapes (jsonParsed `pre/postTokenBalances` owned by the pool, signature pages) follow the public RPC format and the synthetic stream. They are not verified against live services.
