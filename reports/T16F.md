## Decision the coordinator must confirm (read first)

The task asked to "refresh ALL held marks in the same cycle immediately before the entry decision, within the 10 s freshness".
That cannot hold, and I did not implement it, because of arithmetic, not effort:

* An entry is history preparation (<= 18 s) + a bounded cycle (10 s deadline + 2 s sleep) BEFORE the engine decides, i.e. 30 s.
* N held positions need N sequential legs of ~7.8 s (5 requests per leg at 2 s provider pacing).
* The candidate's quote and history must also stay fresh for the same decision, so refreshing marks last just ages the candidate
  (two legs: the quote is 15.6 s old against its 10 s TTL).

So with `price_ttl_seconds = 10` no concurrent entry can ever be accepted (the oldest mark is 38 s old with one held position and 85 s
with seven; `test_timeline_arithmetic_for_every_book_size`). The fix I implemented is a **versioned, opt-in engine rule**:
`paper_portfolio_mark_ttl_seconds` (int, `price_ttl_seconds`..120, valid only with `paper_concurrent_entries_version: 1`, REQUIRED
when the flag is on) replaces the price TTL in exactly two engine checks: the entry-time `STALE_PORTFOLIO` gate and the day-rollover
"all marks current" test. The candidate's own price/quote/mint evidence, exits, quote-exit validation and the stale-mark watchdog still use
10 s. Key absent: byte-identical behaviour. Risk bound (docs/MULTI_POSITION.md): at one entry decision equity is computed from marks up to
120 s old; positions are <= 2 % each and exposure <= 8 %, so equity is overstated by at most 8 % there, never affecting exits.
This is a freshness-gate relaxation for entries, deliberately gated behind an explicit experiment key; AGENTS.md says never to weaken an
evidence gate to create entries, so please treat it as a decision, not a default. Setting the TTL equal to the price TTL restores the
old rule (and then every concurrent entry is refused before any charge).

## What changed, per item
1. **No concurrent entry could ever fill.** Cause as described (marks aged by preparation + quote before the engine decides). Fixed by the
   portfolio TTL rule above and by making the pre-I/O estimate include preparation: `ENTRY_SECONDS = 30` (`PREPARATION_SECONDS` 18, asserted
   equal to `desk.paper_history_preparation.PREPARATION_SECONDS`, + 12 s cycle). The estimate is applied ONLY before the dispatch intent
   (`_preflight(ctx)`); the later calls that carry a scan skip it, because a refusal after charges would leave an unresolved intent (permanent
   latch). Doomed entries are refused with zero requests, zero intents, zero admissions (`ConcurrentEntryRefusedBeforeAnyCharge`).
2. **Entries delay exits.** Not solvable by splitting the lease: `run_once` takes the worker, evidence and ledger locks without waiting, so a
   held pass cannot run inside an entry whatever the scheduler lease does. Implemented: (a) the entry tick already runs the held legs first;
   (b) a held tick that finds the lease taken now WAITS up to 40 s (0.5 s polls, real flock) and runs right after the entry instead of being
   skipped to the next timer tick; flag off or invalid config never waits and is byte-identical (`SCHEDULER_BUSY`). Honest bound: the worst-case
   gap between two refreshes of a position is the held cadence plus the remaining entry duration, not "cadence plus a small margin".
   The earlier doc claim "exits are never starved by entries" was false and is corrected. `HeldLeaseWaitTests` measure the latency
   (starts within one poll of the release) and the bounded wait.
3. **Held-first covered one position.** `plan_legs(positions, None)` now covers every open position, oldest mark first; the monitor service
   default is no cap; the fresh held unit passes `--wall-seconds 64` (8 legs = 62.4 s) and fits `TimeoutStartSec=120` with the 40 s lease wait.
   The entry tick's held pass runs all legs.
4. **Day rollover.** With the portfolio TTL, rollover fires at the first entry decision after midnight because the held-first legs just refreshed
   every mark (`RolloverTests`: three positions across UTC midnight; without the TTL the same book stays on yesterday and the entry is
   rejected). Pure held passes still defer rollover for any book size (each leg's own mark is one cadence old when evaluated); that is
   pre-existing with one position and only affects entry gates, which run after the rollover. Documented.
5. **Restart.** `test_cold_restart_keeps_every_fill_charge_and_monitoring_reservation`: two real dispatcher entries on `fresh_start` stores
   with REAL held legs between them (monitoring cycles, 5 reservations + 5 outcomes each, 15 after the next tick) and `tools.ops.verify_cycle`
   snapshots: restart after each stage compares PASS, the charges are visible as append-only progress, strict comparison FAILs exactly when
   something changed.
6. **Docs.** `docs/MULTI_POSITION.md` rewritten: timeline arithmetic, why the TTL exists and its risk bound, the lease/exit-delay truth,
   rollover semantics, corrected units/wall-time table, and the corrected first setting (2, then 4, with the TTL).

## Limitations (honest)
- Elapsed time is modelled (fixture clock), not measured live; leg 7.8 s and entry 30 s are documented fixture/bound figures.
- The portfolio TTL is a risk relaxation (bound above); the coordinator must approve it for the experiment config.
- Exits can still wait for a running entry (<= its duration); the lease wait covers a typical entry, not the ~282 s code worst case.
- Pure-held rollover deferral is unchanged (pre-existing).
- Two scenarios remain untested: a leg that exits a position during the same tick (engine tests cover exits generally), and `tools/paper_scheduler` entry mode end to end with the real
  monitor service (its pieces are tested separately: held-first order and capture, legs, lease).
- `desk-paper-monitor.timer` must stay disabled (it uses the 10 s TTL and would force `EXIT_ONLY`; T23).
