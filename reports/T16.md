* **`desk/paper_monitor_service.py`**: with the flag, one positions-only cycle per position, unresolved exits then

* **`desk/paper_cycle.py`** (only the per-item held-mint gate, 9 lines): with the flag, supplied position
  targets must be a duplicate-free subset of the open positions; otherwise the original equality rule.
  Candidate-into-held-mint is still skipped per item.

## Important finding — read before activating
The engine rejects any entry whose held marks are older than `price_ttl_seconds` (10 s) at the entry time
(`STALE_PORTFOLIO`). Measured fixture legs are ~7.8 s (SELL) and ~7.0-8 s (BUY). Refreshing N marks and then deciding an
entry therefore fits only for N = 1: 2 held positions already give ~15.8 s for the older mark. The pre-I/O check refuses
such entries with zero requests (instead of ~9 requests then an engine reject). Net effect with today's timings:
`max_positions: 2` is the realistic first setting; 4 needs faster legs, an in-cycle reorder (collect candidate first,
refresh all marks last), or a developer decision on `price_ttl_seconds`. None of these was implemented; the engine gate
was not touched or weakened.

## Limitations
* No live measurement; all timings are the documented fixture figures.
* Deploy units were not edited (outside the files this task owns). The repo's units have entry `TimeoutStartSec=180`
  and held `TimeoutStartSec=20`; the task text said 600 s / 120 s. Four legs (31.2 s) fit the entry unit but not the held
  unit, hence the leg splitting; suggested held unit settings are in the doc.
* Dashboard/ledger-reader per-position view not changed (files not owned); `attribution()` is the reusable read-only core.
* A long-failing oldest (unresolved-exit) leg can consume the single leg slot at the default 12 s budget; raise
  `--wall-seconds` with the unit timeout when more than one position is held.
* Monitoring reservations were not exercised by a real multi-leg budget run; use `tools.ops.verify_cycle` (T06) across a
  restart for charge/duplicate verification.
